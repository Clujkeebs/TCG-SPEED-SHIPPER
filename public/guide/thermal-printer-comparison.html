Rollo vs. Dymo vs. MUNBYN: The Best Thermal Printer for TCGPlayer Sellers

A thermal label printer is one of the best investments a TCGPlayer seller can make — once you're printing instead of handwriting addresses, you save two to five minutes per order. Here's how the popular options actually compare.

Updated September 2026 · 6 min read

All of these print 4×6 direct-thermal labels — no ink, no toner, just heat-sensitive paper — and all of them work with the PDFs TCG Speed Shipper generates from your TCGPlayer packing list. The right one for you mostly comes down to order volume and whether you want wireless.

Rollo Wireless

The most popular choice among high-volume TCG sellers. Wi-Fi connectivity means no USB cable required, and it works from Mac, Windows, iOS, and Android alike — genuinely convenient if you print from a phone or a different room than your desktop. Print quality is crisp, there's zero ink cost, and the Rollo app makes initial setup straightforward. The main tradeoff is price: it's the second most expensive option here.

MUNBYN 4×6

Excellent value for the price, and usually the printer we'd point a new seller toward first. The USB connection is reliable and setup is plug-and-play on most systems — plug it in, install the driver, print. Print quality is very good, close to what you'd get from the Rollo. If you don't need wireless and want to keep costs down while you find out whether a thermal printer is worth it for your volume, this is the low-risk choice.

Dymo LabelWriter 4XL

A long-established workhorse with the broadest software compatibility of anything on this list — if another tool you use only supports Dymo printers by name, this is the safe pick. It's slightly slower than the Rollo and costs more up front. Dymo's own-brand label rolls tend to run more expensive too, but generic 4×6 direct-thermal rolls work fine in it and close most of that gap.

Zebra ZP450 / ZD410

Commercial-grade printers that show up frequently on eBay and Facebook Marketplace at steep discounts, since businesses upgrade and sell their old units. They require slightly more setup — driver hunting, sometimes a serial-to-USB adapter — but they're built to survive years of daily commercial use. A great option if you're comfortable sourcing and configuring used equipment yourself.

Just use a regular printer

You don't need a thermal printer to start. Using TCG Speed Shipper's 8.5×11 format, any inkjet or laser printer works — print 1, 2, 3, or 4 labels per page, cut along the guide lines, and tape them on. It's slower than a thermal setup and costs a little in ink, but it's zero additional hardware cost, which matters if you're not yet sure shipping volume justifies a $80–270 printer.

Print settings that matter regardless of printer: for 4×6 thermal printers, set your paper size to exactly 4×6 inches, set margins to zero (or "borderless"), and disable any "fit to page" scaling. The PDF TCG Speed Shipper generates is already sized to fill a 4×6 label exactly, so scaling only makes it print smaller or cut off. For 8.5×11 printing, choose "Actual Size" in the print dialog rather than "Fit to Page" to keep the cut-line alignment accurate.
