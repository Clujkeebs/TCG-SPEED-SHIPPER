Surviving the Holiday Shipping Rush: Tips for TCGplayer Sellers

Order volume in November and December can run several times a normal month's pace. The sellers who handle it well all prepped in October, not December 20th.

Updated September 2026 · 7 min read

What actually breaks first

It's rarely a single dramatic failure — it's a workflow built for 10 orders a day quietly falling behind at 40. The specific bottlenecks that show up almost every year:

  • Packaging supplies running out mid-rush, when reorder lead times are also longer because every supplier is busier.
  • Manual labeling and address entry that was tolerable at low volume becoming the actual limiting factor on how many orders you can ship per day.
  • Carrier delays that are outside your control but still land on your seller rating if buyers perceive it as your fault.
  • Grading corners getting cut under time pressure — exactly the pattern that leads to condition disputes and suspension risk.

Prep checklist: do this in October, not December

1

Stock up on packaging early. Order 2-3x your normal monthly volume of sleeves, toploaders, team bags, and mailers before the seasonal rush starts. See what to stock and in what ratio.

2

Fix your labeling bottleneck before volume triples, not during. If you're still hand-typing addresses from a packing list, that's the step that will hurt most at holiday volume — automate it now while there's no pressure. TCG Speed Shipper turns a CSV export into printed labels in seconds regardless of order count.

3

Consider a thermal printer if you haven't already. The time saved per label compounds fast at holiday volume — see the Rollo vs. Dymo vs. MUNBYN comparison if you're still printing on a regular printer.

4

Widen your handling time honestly, if needed. A slightly longer stated handling time that you consistently beat is better for your rating than an aggressive one you miss under pressure. See how late shipping actually gets flagged.

5

Set aside more working capital than usual. Higher volume means more cash tied up in packaging and postage before payouts land — see the payout schedule and plan the gap accordingly.

During the rush: protect the fundamentals over the extras

When volume spikes, the temptation is to cut corners on the things that feel optional — careful grading, double-checking an address, packaging that's technically "good enough." These are exactly the wrong things to compress. If something has to give, let it be response time on non-urgent messages or how quickly you relist sold-out inventory — not condition accuracy or packing quality.

The sellers who come out of the holidays with their rating intact are almost always the ones who fixed their slowest step — usually labeling — before the volume arrived, rather than trying to power through it manually at 4x the normal pace.
