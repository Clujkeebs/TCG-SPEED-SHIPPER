How to Start Selling on TCGplayer: A Beginner's Guide

Everything that actually matters in your first 30 days as a seller — not the marketing copy version, the "here's what will trip you up" version.

Updated September 2026 · 9 min read

Step 1: Decide what you're actually selling

TCGplayer supports Magic: The Gathering, Pokémon, Yu-Gi-Oh!, Lorcana, One Piece, Digimon, Flesh and Blood, and several smaller games. New sellers do best specializing early — pick the game you already know the market for, since pricing mistakes are the single biggest thing that costs new sellers money (more on that in pricing your cards to sell fast). You can always expand into a second game once your first is running smoothly.

Step 2: Create your seller account

1

Go to TCGplayer's seller portal and register as an individual seller — this is free and doesn't require a business license to start.

2

Fill in your payout details (bank account for direct deposit) and your ship-from address.

3

You'll start at Seller Level 1. That's normal — see how seller levels work for what changes as you climb.

Step 3: List your first cards

Search the card in TCGplayer's catalog, select the correct set and condition, and enter your price and quantity. Condition accuracy matters more than almost anything else for new sellers — grade conservatively. A card graded "Near Mint" that arrives with a corner ding generates a complaint that follows your feedback rating; a card graded "Lightly Played" that arrives looking better than expected just makes a buyer happy. When in doubt, grade down.

Step 4: Get your shipping workflow sorted before orders start coming in

This is the step new sellers skip and regret. Once you have live listings, orders can arrive at any time, and TCGplayer expects orders shipped within your stated handling time — usually 1-2 business days. Before you list anything, have on hand:

  • Penny sleeves and semi-rigid toploaders or a bubble mailer, depending on order value (see the packaging supplies guide)
  • A way to print addresses — even a home printer and plain paper works for your first few orders
  • A plan for tracking, once your order volume justifies the cost (see what shipping actually costs)

When your first orders come in, TCGplayer generates a packing list you can export as a CSV. Turning that into an actual printed label is its own small project — TCG Speed Shipper does it in under a minute, free for your first 10 labels a month, if you'd rather not build a spreadsheet macro for it.

Step 5: Ship fast and pack carefully for your first 20-30 orders

Your seller rating is built almost entirely from your earliest transactions, when a single mistake is a larger percentage of your total feedback. Ship on time, pack so a card can't shift and bend in transit, and communicate if something is genuinely going to be late. This period is also where the seeds of a future suspension usually get planted or avoided — mis-graded cards and slow shipping compound fast.

Common first-week mistake: pricing everything at exact market price and wondering why nothing sells. Market price is where the last sale happened, not where the next one will. New listings usually need to undercut slightly to get initial momentum and seller-rating traction — see the pricing guide for the actual mechanics.

What to expect financially in month one

TCGplayer charges 8.5% marketplace fee plus a 2.5% payment processing fee plus $0.50 per order — budget for roughly 11-12% off the top before you've spent a cent on packaging or postage. Payouts land on a schedule, not instantly; see how long TCGplayer actually takes to pay so you're not caught short on cash flow while packaging costs come out of pocket immediately.
