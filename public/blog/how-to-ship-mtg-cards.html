How to Ship MTG Cards

A single Magic card, a playset, a foil, and a box of bulk each need a slightly different packing plan. Start with protection, then choose postage and tracking for the order.

Published September 28, 2026 · 6 min read

Magic singles ship differently from playsets, bulk, or expensive cards. A raw single can fit a plain white envelope (PWE) when it is protected and the completed mailpiece qualifies; a stack needs a box, and an expensive order may need tracking or a signature. Here is how to pack each without guessing postage or policy thresholds.

Also shipping Pokémon? See How to Ship Pokémon Cards for the same single-card and bulk workflow for another game.

The short answer

  • One low-value single: penny sleeve, toploader, painter’s tape, and a cardboard stiffener inside a plain white envelope.
  • A playset or several cards: keep the cards together in sleeves or a team bag, then protect the stack from bending and movement.
  • Bulk or 100+ cards: use a snug box, weigh the fully packed parcel, and choose a suitable tracked parcel service.
  • Expensive cards: use tracking; TCGplayer’s seller guidance calls for signature confirmation at its high-value threshold.

TCGplayer’s shipping guide recommends tracking over $20, requires it at $49.99 and up, and requires signature confirmation at $250 and up. Check the seller shipping thresholds and current TCGplayer guidance.

How to ship a single MTG card in a plain white envelope

For a suitable low-value raw single, keep it clean and flat without letting tape touch the card. Pack it this way:

1

Sleeve. Put the Magic card in a penny sleeve.

2

Topload. Slide the sleeved card into a rigid toploader.

3

Secure. Use a small strip of painter’s tape across the opening or place the toploader in a team bag. Keep adhesive away from the card.

4

Stiffen. Sandwich the protected card between thin cardboard or a folded index card.

5

Address. Put the bundle in the envelope, seal it, and apply postage for the complete mailpiece.

For envelope folds and USPS shape checks, see the plain white envelope guide.

How many stamps does a Magic card need?

Stamp count depends on the complete mailpiece, not just the number of cards. Weigh the sealed envelope and check current letter postage and machinability rules in the trading-card stamp guide, which links to USPS Notice 123 and the Domestic Mail Manual. More stamps do not change a mailpiece’s classification.

When to use tracking or Ground Advantage

Use order value, not the game or card count, to decide how much visibility you need. The TCGplayer seller-guide wording is:

TCGplayer order valueGuidancePractical option
Under $20Tracking is not required.A stamped PWE may suit a low-value single.
Over $20 to $49.98Tracking is recommended for your own protection.Choose a PWE or a tracked service based on the order.
$49.99 and upTracking or delivery confirmation is required.Use a tracked parcel service such as USPS Ground Advantage.
$250 and upTracking and signature confirmation are required.Use a tracked parcel with signature confirmation.

These are TCGplayer thresholds. A plain stamp is not tracking. Ground Advantage is a practical tracked-parcel option; compare the service and current postage for the packed shipment. See the full seller shipping guide.

Shipping expensive MTG cards

Protect an expensive raw single with a sleeve, toploader, and team bag, then use a bubble mailer or snug box and add tracking according to the order value. TCGplayer orders of $250 and up also need signature confirmation.

For a slab, follow the graded-card shipping guide. If tracking shows a missing delivery, see the lost-package steps for TCGplayer sellers.

Shipping playsets, bulk, and 100+ cards

For a playset or several singles, keep each card sleeved and align the group inside a team bag or other secure layer. For bulk or 100+ cards, use a snug box, protect the corners, and fill open space so the stack cannot slide. Weigh the packed box and choose postage for that parcel; Ground Advantage is one tracked option. Check the order value too: TCGplayer’s tracking and signature thresholds still apply.

How to protect foil Magic cards

Keep a foil in a sleeve and toploader, then pack it so it cannot slide or bend. Use a stiffener in a PWE or cushioning in a parcel, and avoid exposing foils to humidity or heat while sorting and packing. Separate multiple cards with sleeves; choose tracking based on order value, not card thickness.

Common MTG shipping mistakes

  • Sending a raw single without a sleeve, toploader, and stiffener.
  • Putting adhesive where it can touch the card or make the buyer cut close to it.
  • Guessing stamp postage from card count instead of weighing the fully packed envelope.
  • Putting a valuable order in a PWE even though its TCGplayer value calls for tracking or a signature.
  • Letting bulk shift in a box or leaving a foil unprotected during packing.

FAQ

How do you ship MTG cards safely?

Sleeve the card, add a toploader and cardboard stiffener, and use a stamped PWE for a suitable low-value order. Choose tracking when value or marketplace guidance calls for it.

How many stamps do I need to mail a Magic card?

There is no reliable stamp count based only on card count. Weigh the sealed envelope and check current USPS postage and machinability rules in the stamp guide.

How do you ship bulk MTG cards or 100 cards?

Use a snug box, secure the stack, and fill open space. Weigh the parcel and choose a tracked service such as USPS Ground Advantage.

How do you ship expensive MTG cards?

TCGplayer orders of $49.99 and up need tracking; orders of $250 and up also need signature confirmation. See the graded-card guide for a slab.

How do you ship MTG cards on eBay?

Use a sleeve, toploader, and stiffener. This guide’s value thresholds are for TCGplayer orders; check eBay’s current guidance for service selection.

How should I protect foil MTG cards when shipping?

Keep the foil in a sleeve and toploader, pack it so it cannot slide, and avoid humidity or heat.
