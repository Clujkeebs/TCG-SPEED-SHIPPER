How to Ship Pokémon Cards

The right way to ship a Pokémon card depends almost entirely on what it's worth. Here's the packing and service to use at every price point.

Published September 27, 2026 · 7 min read

Most Pokémon card orders are small: a few singles for a deck or a binder. Shipping them well is about two things: the card arrives flat and unbent, and you don't spend more on postage than the order is worth. Here's the method at each price point.

Shipping Magic instead? See How to Ship MTG Cards for the singles, bulk, and high-value-card workflow.

The short answer

  • Under about $20: penny sleeve, toploader, team bag, in a plain white envelope (PWE) with a stamp.
  • $20 and up: same packing, but in a bubble mailer with tracking (USPS Ground Advantage).
  • $250 and up: tracked bubble mailer or small box, plus signature confirmation.
  • Bulk and sealed product: a snug box, void fill, and tracking.

If you sell on TCGplayer, its rules set some of these lines for you: tracking is required at $49.99 and signature confirmation at $250. See TCGplayer Shipping Guidelines.

How to pack a single card

1

Penny sleeve. Slide the card in top-first so the edges don't catch.

2

Toploader. Put the sleeved card into a 3×4 toploader. For multiple cards, stack a few sleeved cards in one toploader or use a card saver.

3

Close the top. A team bag over the toploader, or a small strip of painter's tape across the opening, stops the card sliding out. Avoid regular tape on the toploader: buyers hate cutting it off.

4

Stiffen it. For a PWE, sandwich the toploader between two pieces of thin cardboard, or wrap it in a folded index card, so the envelope can't bend in the sorting machines.

5

Envelope and label. Put it in the envelope, write or print the address, add the order number, and stamp it.

Plain white envelope (PWE) vs. bubble mailer

A PWE with a stamp is the cheapest way to send a card, but it has no tracking and no insurance. That's fine for low-value singles and a real risk for anything a buyer would open a claim over. A rigid envelope can also be charged the USPS nonmachinable surcharge; the details are in How to Ship Trading Cards in a Plain White Envelope.

A bubble mailer with a Ground Advantage label costs more but gives you tracking, which is your proof of delivery if a buyer says a package never arrived. Compare real costs in How Much Does It Cost to Ship Trading Cards?

High-value cards ($100+)

  • Use a card saver or a snug toploader, then a team bag, then bubble wrap around it.
  • Ship in a bubble mailer or small box, never a PWE.
  • Add tracking, and signature confirmation at $250+ (required on TCGplayer). Consider insurance for anything you couldn't afford to refund.
  • Take a quick photo of the card and the packed mailer before you seal it. If a claim ever happens, you'll be glad you did.

For a card that has already been graded, use the separate graded-card shipping guide—a slab needs different protection from a raw single.

Bulk lots and sealed product

Bulk commons go in a snug box, with the cards rubber-banded loosely or in a bag, and paper or bubble wrap filling the gaps so nothing shifts. Sealed booster boxes and ETBs need a box with an inch of padding on every side: a crushed corner can knock real value off a sealed product.

Common mistakes

  • Shipping a card loose in an envelope with no toploader.
  • Taping the toploader shut so the buyer has to cut near the card.
  • Sending a $60 card in a PWE to save a couple of dollars, then refunding it when it "never arrived".
  • Forgetting the order number, so the buyer can't match the package to their order.

FAQ

What is the cheapest way to ship a Pokémon card?

A plain white envelope with a Forever stamp: the card in a penny sleeve and toploader, stiffened with thin cardboard. It has no tracking, so use it only for low-value cards.

Do I need a toploader to ship Pokémon cards?

For singles, yes. A penny sleeve alone doesn't stop a card bending in the mail. A toploader or card saver is what keeps it flat.

When should I add tracking?

Whenever the card is worth more than you'd want to refund. On TCGplayer, tracking is required at $49.99 and signature confirmation at $250.

Can I ship Pokémon cards with Media Mail?

No. Media Mail is only for things like books and recorded media, and trading cards don't qualify. See Can You Ship Trading Cards Media Mail?
