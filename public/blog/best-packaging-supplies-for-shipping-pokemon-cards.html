Best Packaging Supplies for Shipping Pokémon Cards

The same principles apply to any TCG, but Pokémon's mix of bulk commons and expensive chase cards makes the "what level of protection does this order need" question especially concrete.

Updated September 2026 · 7 min read

The core supply list

🛡️
Penny sleeves
~$4-6 per 100
The baseline for every single card, regardless of value. No reason to ship a card without one.
📇
Semi-rigid toploaders
~$8-15 per 100
Prevents bending. Use for anything above bulk value — roughly $2+ per card is a reasonable line.
📦
Team bags / card savers
~$5-8 per 100
Keeps a sleeved-and-toploadered card from sliding around inside a toploader — cheap insurance against rattling damage.
✉️
Rigid mailers / bubble mailers
~$0.25-0.60 each
For anything with a toploader inside — a plain envelope will bend under normal mail handling.
🖨️
4x6 thermal labels
~$0.03-0.05 each
See the printer comparison for which setup fits your volume.

Package by order value, not by habit

Order valueRecommended packaging
Bulk / commons, under ~$2Penny sleeve, plain envelope — tracked if TCGplayer or your policy requires it, but no toploader needed
Standard singles, ~$2-25Penny sleeve + toploader + team bag, in a rigid or bubble mailer
Higher-value singles, $25-100+Same as above, plus tracking and enough padding that the toploader can't shift inside the envelope
Graded slabs, sealed productA properly sized box, not an envelope — slabs crack in mailers under normal transit pressure

The most common packaging mistake

Shipping a toploadered card in a plain paper envelope. Postal sorting machines apply real pressure and can bend a rigid toploader corner right through a standard envelope — the toploader protects the card from bending on its own, but does nothing if the whole package bends around it. A rigid mailer or a well-padded bubble mailer solves this for a few extra cents per order, which is nearly always worth it once you weigh it against a damage complaint.

Overkill is a real cost too. Double-boxing a $1 bulk common, or using a full rigid mailer for a penny sleeve, adds material cost and package weight (which can bump your postage tier) without meaningfully reducing risk. Match protection to value — see the real cost breakdown for how these choices show up in your margin.

Buying supplies in bulk without overcommitting

Penny sleeves and toploaders are cheap enough per-unit that buying in bulk (boxes of 1,000+) usually pays for itself within your first few weeks of consistent selling. Team bags and mailers are worth watching more carefully — buy a mid-size quantity first and see what your actual order-value mix looks like before committing to a bulk order sized for a volume you might not hit yet.
