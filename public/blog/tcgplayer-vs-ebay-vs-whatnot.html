TCGplayer vs. eBay vs. Whatnot: Where Should You Sell Your Cards?

Each platform rewards a different kind of selling. Most sellers who do real volume end up on at least two of the three, not just one.

Updated September 2026 · 8 min read

The short version

  • TCGplayer — best for singles at scale, a built-in buyer audience actively searching for specific cards, and the least time spent per sale.
  • eBay — best for graded cards, sealed product, and anything where photos and a compelling listing meaningfully affect the price.
  • Whatnot — best for building a repeat-buyer audience through live selling, at the cost of your time being the product.

Fee percentages shift over time on every platform — check current rates before making a decision based on this alone. The bigger differentiator is usually audience and effort, not the fee gap.

Where TCGplayer wins

Buyers come to TCGplayer already searching for a specific card. You don't need to write a compelling listing, take great photos, or build an audience — you need an accurate price and condition grade. That makes it the most time-efficient platform per dollar of revenue for straightforward singles, especially at volume. It's also the platform with the clearest path to a level and reputation system that compounds over time.

Where eBay wins

eBay's buyer base is broader and less price-sensitive for unique or high-value items — a well-photographed graded slab or a sealed booster box often does better there than as a plain catalog listing on TCGplayer, because eBay lets you tell a story around the item. The tradeoff is real time cost: good photos, a written description, and active listing management.

Where Whatnot wins

Whatnot trades your time for a direct relationship with buyers. Live selling lets you move bulk and mid-value cards fast, build repeat customers who show up to every stream, and price dynamically in the moment. It's genuinely a different skill from listing management — closer to running a small live show — and it doesn't scale the same way a catalog of static listings does.

Why most serious sellers use more than one

A common, sustainable split: bulk and mid-value singles on TCGplayer where buyers are already searching, high-value and graded pieces on eBay where presentation pays off, and Whatnot for building an audience and moving inventory that doesn't sell well as a static listing on either. Running two or three platforms means juggling separate shipping workflows too — which is exactly the kind of overhead that makes tools like TCG Speed Shipper worth having just for the TCGplayer side, so that piece of the operation stops eating your time.

One thing that doesn't change across platforms: shipping mistakes cost you the same way everywhere — slow handling and sloppy packing hurt your reputation whether it's TCGplayer feedback, eBay seller rating, or Whatnot reviews.
