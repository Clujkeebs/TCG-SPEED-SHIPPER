Can You Ship Trading Cards Media Mail?

Media Mail looks like a cheap way to ship a box of cards. It isn't allowed, and USPS can make you (or your buyer) pay the difference.

Published September 27, 2026 · 4 min read

It's a common question when you're shipping a heavy lot: Media Mail is cheap, so can a box of cards go that way? No. Trading cards don't qualify for USPS Media Mail.

What Media Mail is for

USPS Media Mail is restricted to educational and media materials: books, sound recordings, recorded video, printed music, printed educational charts, and computer-readable media, among a few others. Trading cards, including Pokémon, Magic, Yu-Gi-Oh! and sports cards, are collectibles, not books or recorded media, so they aren't eligible. Neither are booster boxes or other sealed product.

What happens if you use it anyway

Media Mail packages can be opened and inspected. If USPS finds ineligible contents, the package can be returned to you or delivered with postage due charged to the recipient. Either one means an unhappy buyer and a possible bad review or claim. It isn't worth the saving.

What to use instead

  • A few low-value singles: a plain white envelope with a stamp. See how to ship in a PWE.
  • Anything you want tracked: a bubble mailer with USPS Ground Advantage.
  • Heavy lots and sealed product: Ground Advantage by weight, or a Priority Mail flat-rate box when the lot is dense and heavy. Compare both before buying the label.
  • On eBay: eBay Standard Envelope for eligible lower-priced raw cards.

FAQ

Can Pokémon cards be shipped Media Mail?

No. Media Mail is limited to items like books and recorded media, and trading cards don't qualify.

What happens if I ship cards Media Mail?

USPS can inspect the package, then return it or charge postage due to the recipient.

What's the cheapest legitimate way to ship a heavy card lot?

Usually USPS Ground Advantage by weight. For very dense, heavy lots, compare it with a Priority Mail flat-rate box.
